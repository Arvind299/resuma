Buddhism

Buddhism is a spiritual tradition that focuses on personal spiritual development and the attainment of a deep insight into the true nature of life. It was founded by Siddhartha Gautama, known as the Buddha, in the 6th century BCE in India. The teachings of Buddhism emphasize mindfulness, meditation, and ethical living. Central concepts include the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path, which guide practitioners toward enlightenment and the cessation of suffering.

Jainism

Jainism is an ancient Indian religion that emphasizes non-violence, truth, and asceticism. It was founded by Lord Mahavira in the 6th century BCE, though its origins trace back further to earlier Tirthankaras. Jainism teaches a path of spiritual purity and enlightenment through disciplined practices such as strict vegetarianism, non-possessiveness, and self-discipline. The concept of Ahimsa (non-violence) is central to Jain philosophy, promoting respect and compassion for all living beings.
